Research

One question runs through my work: how do we make a robot's actions depend on the task it was given, rather than the scenes it has memorized?

Knowing Isn’t Following: Where the Instruction Stops Mattering in a Vision-Language-Action Policy

Yi-Hsiu Lee, Tzu-Yu Chuang, Ching-Hsiang Chang, Yi-Ting Chen, Min Sun, Yuan-Fu Yang · under review, ICRA 2027

One-stage autoregressive VLAs — action tokens inside the language-modeling stream — follow instructions far better than a two-stage baseline (82.2% vs. 0/180 on held-out task recombinations). Yet they still inherit scene-bound shortcuts: in one setting the model names the correct target while grasping the familiar object in 19 of 20 trials. By probing internal activations we localize where the instruction stops influencing the action pathway, and show that a targeted intervention on that representation flips the behavior.

Semantic Re-anchoring for Hybrid Manipulation Policies

Yi-Hsiu Lee, Tzu-Yu Chuang, Ching-Hsiang Chang, Yung-Hsuan Lo, Yi-Ting Chen, Min Sun, Yuan-Fu Yang · under review, WACV 2027

A VLM grounds the target and guides the approach while a wrist-view, delta-action policy performs the local interaction — restoring manipulation across changed object placements where an end-to-end policy breaks. Its limitation (two separately engineered stages) is what pushed me toward studying how semantics couple to action inside one policy.

Ongoing: in-context imitation learning

Can a policy infer a new task’s structure from a few demonstrations at inference time, reusing its manipulation skills without retraining? I am studying training objectives and counterfactual evaluations that force a policy to read the demonstration rather than the scene — continuing the mechanistic thread of Knowing Isn’t Following.
